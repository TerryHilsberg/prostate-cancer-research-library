# LDL-Lowering Medicines, Statins, PCSK9 Inhibitors, and Advanced Prostate Cancer: Evidence Map

**Retrieved:** 2026-07-23  
**Category:** Advanced/recurrent disease; metabolic therapy; cardiovascular risk during prostate-cancer treatment

!!! warning "Educational research note — not medical advice"
    Statins, ezetimibe, and PCSK9 inhibitors are established cardiovascular-risk medicines, not established prostate-cancer treatments. Patients should not start, stop, or change lipid-lowering therapy for cancer-control reasons without their oncology, GP, and/or cardio-oncology team. The strongest current clinical reason to treat LDL cholesterol in many prostate-cancer patients is cardiovascular risk reduction, especially during androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) and androgen-receptor pathway inhibitor therapy.

## Patient-facing bottom line

## Why LDL and lipid metabolism are being studied in advanced prostate cancer

Advanced prostate cancer is strongly shaped by androgen-receptor signalling. Cholesterol and related lipids matter because:

- cholesterol is a precursor for steroid hormones and intratumoural androgen synthesis;
- prostate cancer cells can reprogram lipid uptake, synthesis, storage, and sphingolipid metabolism;
- ADT and androgen-receptor pathway inhibitors can worsen cardiometabolic risk, including weight gain, insulin resistance, dyslipidaemia, and cardiovascular events;
- statins may have non-LDL effects, including effects on mevalonate-pathway signalling, inflammation, membrane biology, and tumour-cell transporters;
- PCSK9 inhibitors profoundly lower LDL cholesterol and may also alter selected lipid species, but their anticancer role remains investigational.

A particularly important mechanistic observation is that some statins can compete with dehydroepiandrosterone sulfate (DHEAS), an androgen precursor, for uptake via the **SLCO2B1** transporter. This gives a plausible explanation for why statins might interact with ADT response, but plausibility is not the same as proof of clinical benefit.

## Evidence synthesis by evidence lane

### 1) Statins plus ADT: consistent observational survival signal, but not definitive proof

The most consistent clinical signal is in men with advanced prostate cancer receiving ADT or androgen-ablative therapy. Large observational cohorts and meta-analyses report lower overall mortality and prostate-cancer-specific mortality among statin users. However, these are mostly non-randomized data. Statin users may differ from non-users in cardiovascular care, healthcare access, baseline cholesterol, comorbidities, screening intensity, and other confounders.

**Patient interpretation:** if there is already a cardiovascular indication for a statin, the prostate-cancer literature gives additional reassurance and a plausible research rationale. It does **not** yet prove that statins should be prescribed solely as anticancer therapy.

### 2) Statins with androgen-receptor pathway inhibitors: promising but inconsistent

In mCRPC treated with abiraterone or enzalutamide after docetaxel, the international STABEN retrospective study found longer survival and higher early PSA-decline rates among statin users. A smaller abiraterone-specific retrospective study also found longer overall survival in statin users.

More recent secondary analyses from large randomized drug trials are more mixed. A 2025 pooled individual-patient analysis of TITAN and SPARTAN reported better overall survival among statin-exposed patients receiving apalutamide, but also higher grade ≥3 cardiac adverse events among statin users, probably reflecting higher baseline cardiovascular risk. Separate ARAMIS and SPARTAN secondary analyses did **not** show a consistent overall benefit from statin use. This mixed picture is exactly why prospective randomized trials are needed.

### 3) PCSK9 inhibitors: Australia is testing a biomarker-first hypothesis

PCSK9 inhibitors such as **evolocumab** and **alirocumab** are proven LDL-lowering cardiovascular medicines. Their prostate-cancer role is not established. The most directly relevant prostate-cancer study is an Australian open-label phase II trial of **evolocumab** in men with mCRPC whose plasma lipid profile is positive for **PCPro**, a poor-prognosis sphingolipid-based biomarker.

The trial asks an early translational question: can evolocumab safely reverse the poor-prognosis PCPro lipid signature while men continue standard mCRPC treatment? It is **not yet** a survival trial and should not be interpreted as proof that PCSK9 inhibitors improve prostate-cancer outcomes.

### 4) Cardiovascular risk is not secondary — it is part of prostate-cancer care

ADT, abiraterone/prednisone, enzalutamide/apalutamide/darolutamide-class therapy, age, obesity, diabetes, hypertension, smoking, and pre-existing cardiovascular disease can all interact. LDL lowering may therefore be important even if the anticancer hypothesis is unproven.

A RADICAL-PC analysis, which included men from Canada, Israel, Brazil, and Australia, found poor control of cardiovascular risk factors was common in men with prostate cancer. Not taking a statin was one correlate of poor overall cardiovascular-risk-factor control. For many patients, the practical first step is not experimental anticancer statin use, but rigorous cardiovascular prevention.

## Selected published studies and protocols

### Statin use and survival among men receiving androgen-ablative therapies — systematic review and meta-analysis

- **Citation:** Roy S, et al. *JAMA Network Open*. 2022.
- **PMID:** [36449294](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36449294/)
- **DOI:** [10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2022.42676](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2022.42676)
- **Evidence type:** Systematic review and meta-analysis of observational cohorts.
- **Population:** 25 cohorts, 119,878 men receiving ADT or androgen-receptor axis-targeted therapies.
- **Main finding:** Concurrent statin use was associated with lower overall mortality (HR 0.73) and prostate-cancer-specific mortality (HR 0.65). Subgroup analyses suggested a stronger prostate-cancer-specific mortality association in men receiving androgen-receptor axis-targeted therapy than ADT alone.
- **Limitations:** Observational studies, high heterogeneity, possible residual confounding; GRADE certainty rated low.
- **Patient relevance:** Strong signal, but not proof that starting a statin for cancer control improves survival.

### Statin use at ADT initiation and time to progression in hormone-sensitive prostate cancer

- **Citation:** Harshman LC, et al. *JAMA Oncology*. 2015.
- **PMID:** [26181260](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26181260/)
- **DOI:** [10.1001/jamaoncol.2015.0829](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamaoncol.2015.0829)
- **Evidence type:** Mechanistic/in vitro work plus retrospective clinical cohort.
- **Population:** 926 men receiving ADT for biochemical recurrence, metastatic recurrence, or de novo metastatic prostate cancer.
- **Main finding:** Statins inhibited DHEAS uptake through SLCO2B1 in vitro. Clinically, statin use at ADT initiation was associated with longer time to progression during ADT.
- **Limitations:** Retrospective clinical component; statin use not randomized.
- **Patient relevance:** Provides a plausible androgen-transport mechanism linking statins and ADT response.

### Large VA observational study of statins plus ADT in advanced prostate cancer

- **Citation:** Anderson-Carter I, Posielski N, Liou JI, et al. *Urologic Oncology*. 2019.
- **PMID:** [30528885](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30528885/)
- **DOI:** [10.1016/j.urolonc.2018.11.017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.urolonc.2018.11.017)
- **Evidence type:** Large observational cohort using the US Veterans Affairs database.
- **Population:** 87,346 men with prostate cancer treated with ADT for at least 6 months.
- **Main finding:** Statin use was associated with improved overall survival, cancer-specific survival, and longer time to skeletal-related events after adjustment.
- **Limitations:** Non-randomized; residual confounding and healthy-user/care-intensity bias remain possible.
- **Patient relevance:** Supports asking about cardiovascular risk optimization during ADT.

### Post hoc analysis of an intermittent versus continuous ADT randomized trial

- **Citation:** Hamilton RJ, Ding K, Crook JM, et al. *European Urology*. 2021.
- **PMID:** [33390282](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33390282/)
- **DOI:** [10.1016/j.eururo.2020.12.031](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eururo.2020.12.031)
- **Evidence type:** Post hoc secondary analysis of a randomized ADT strategy trial; statin exposure was observational.
- **Population:** 1,364 men starting ADT after primary/salvage radiotherapy.
- **Main finding:** Statin use was associated with lower overall and prostate-cancer-specific mortality; there was a trend toward longer time to castration resistance.
- **Limitations:** Statin use was not randomized, so causal inference is limited.
- **Patient relevance:** Useful because it examines men initiating ADT, but still not a statin RCT.

### STABEN: statins with abiraterone or enzalutamide after docetaxel in mCRPC

- **Citation:** Gordon JA, Buonerba C, Pond G, et al. *Oncotarget*. 2018.
- **PMID:** [29731989](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29731989/)
- **Evidence type:** International retrospective observational study.
- **Population:** 598 men with mCRPC receiving abiraterone or enzalutamide after docetaxel failure; 199 used statins.
- **Main finding:** Statin use was associated with longer overall survival and increased odds of early PSA decline.
- **Limitations:** Retrospective; statin users may differ systematically from non-users; treatment-era and confounding issues.
- **Patient relevance:** Hypothesis-generating for statin/AR-targeted therapy interaction.

### Abiraterone-specific retrospective study in mCRPC

- **Citation:** Di Lorenzo G, Sonpavde G, Pond G, et al. *European Urology Focus*. 2018.
- **PMID:** [28753882](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28753882/)
- **DOI:** [10.1016/j.euf.2017.03.015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.euf.2017.03.015)
- **Evidence type:** Multicentre retrospective cohort.
- **Population:** 187 men with mCRPC receiving abiraterone.
- **Main finding:** Statin use was associated with longer overall survival and higher PSA-decline rates.
- **Limitations:** Retrospective and relatively small.
- **Patient relevance:** Suggests a possible interaction with abiraterone, but not enough to prescribe statins solely for cancer control.

### Statin use in TITAN and SPARTAN apalutamide trials

- **Citation:** Roy S, Ozay ZI, Guha A, et al. *JAMA Network Open*. 2025.
- **PMID:** [40833694](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40833694/)
- **DOI:** [10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2025.27988](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2025.27988)
- **Evidence type:** Individual-patient-data cohort analysis within two phase 3 randomized trials; statin use was observational.
- **Population:** TITAN metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer and SPARTAN nonmetastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer.
- **Main finding:** Statin exposure was associated with superior overall survival in apalutamide-treated patients in both trials, but not clearly in placebo-treated patients. Statin users had higher rates of grade ≥3 cardiac adverse events, likely reflecting underlying cardiovascular risk.
- **Limitations:** Statin exposure not randomized; cardiovascular-risk confounding remains.
- **Patient relevance:** Reinforces the need for cardio-oncology/cardiovascular risk management when using intensified hormonal therapy.

### ARAMIS darolutamide secondary analysis

- **Citation:** Chavarriaga J, Lajkosz K, Sangole N, et al. *Urologic Oncology*. 2025.
- **PMID:** [39477770](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39477770/)
- **DOI:** [10.1016/j.urolonc.2024.08.023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.urolonc.2024.08.023)
- **Evidence type:** Propensity-matched secondary analysis of the ARAMIS trial.
- **Population:** Nonmetastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer treated with darolutamide or placebo.
- **Main finding:** Statin use was not associated with improved metastasis-free survival or secondary outcomes overall.
- **Limitations:** Secondary observational analysis inside an RCT; not a randomized statin comparison.
- **Patient relevance:** A caution against assuming all statin/AR-inhibitor combinations are beneficial.

### SPARTAN statin-use secondary analysis

- **Citation:** Mousa A, Chavarriaga J, Lajkosz K, et al. *Urologic Oncology*. 2026.
- **PMID:** [41654482](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41654482/)
- **DOI:** [10.1016/j.urolonc.2026.110992](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.urolonc.2026.110992)
- **Evidence type:** Secondary analysis of SPARTAN.
- **Population:** Nonmetastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer.
- **Main finding:** Statin use was not associated with improved outcomes overall, with a treatment-arm interaction that requires prospective study rather than clinical overinterpretation.
- **Limitations:** Observational statin exposure; possible imbalance and confounding.
- **Patient relevance:** Adds uncertainty to the statin-as-anticancer-therapy hypothesis.

### Australian simvastatin lipidomic study in mCRPC

- **Citation:** Mak B, Lin HM, Duong T, et al. *Cancers*. 2022.
- **PMID:** [36230715](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36230715/)
- **DOI:** [10.3390/cancers14194792](https://doi.org/10.3390/cancers14194792)
- **Evidence type:** Small single-arm translational study.
- **Population:** 27 men with mCRPC not already on lipid-lowering therapy, given simvastatin 40 mg daily for 12 weeks with standard mCRPC treatment.
- **Main finding:** In men with a poor-prognosis circulating lipidomic signature at baseline, simvastatin shifted some patients to a more favourable lipidomic profile and reduced selected sphingolipids independent of LDL-cholesterol changes.
- **Limitations:** Small, non-randomized, biomarker endpoint; not powered to prove survival or disease-control benefit.
- **Patient relevance:** Australian mechanistic bridge from statin observations to PCSK9/evolocumab biomarker trials.

## Ongoing and recent trials

### Australia: evolocumab in mCRPC using PCPro lipid biomarker

- **Trial/protocol:** Mellor R, Ardolino L, Scheinberg T, et al. “Evolocumab in metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer: study protocol for a single-arm, phase II trial, and initial experience with use of a validated lipid biomarker to direct therapy.” *Therapeutic Advances in Medical Oncology*. 2024.
- **PMID:** [39691585](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39691585/)
- **DOI:** [10.1177/17588359241307814](https://doi.org/10.1177/17588359241307814)
- **Registry:** Australian New Zealand Clinical Trials Registry **ACTRN12622001003763**.
- **Sites listed in the published protocol:** Chris O'Brien Lifehouse, St Vincent's Hospital, and Westmead Hospital, New South Wales, Australia.
- **Design:** Multicentre, open-label, single-arm phase II.
- **Population:** Men with mCRPC commencing docetaxel, cabazitaxel, abiraterone, enzalutamide, olaparib, or Lu-177 PSMA for disease progression, screened for the PCPro lipid biomarker.
- **Intervention:** Evolocumab 420 mg subcutaneously every 4 weeks for 12 weeks, concurrent with standard anticancer therapy.
- **Primary endpoint:** Reversal of the PCPro poor-prognosis lipid signature after 12 weeks.
- **What it can and cannot answer:** It can test biomarker modulation and safety; it cannot by itself prove improved survival.

### Finland/Estonia: atorvastatin phase III trial after ADT initiation

- **Trial/protocol:** ESTO2 / NCT04026230. “Randomised double-blind phase 3 clinical study testing impact of atorvastatin on prostate cancer progression after initiation of androgen deprivation therapy.” *BMJ Open*. 2022.
- **PMID:** [35487730](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35487730/)
- **DOI:** [10.1136/bmjopen-2021-050264](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2021-050264)
- **Registry:** [NCT04026230](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04026230)
- **Design:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase III.
- **Population:** 400 men with de novo metastatic prostate cancer or recurrent disease after primary treatment, starting ADT.
- **Intervention:** Atorvastatin 80 mg daily versus placebo.
- **Primary endpoint:** Time to development of castration resistance.
- **Australian involvement:** I did not find Australian sites for this trial; the protocol lists Finland and Estonia ethics approvals.

### Finland: MOVES trial of exercise and atorvastatin in metastatic cancers

- **Registry:** [NCT05796973](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05796973)
- **Title:** Measuring Oncological Value of Exercise and Statin.
- **Status at search:** Recruiting on ClinicalTrials.gov.
- **Population:** Includes metastatic prostate cancer as well as metastatic breast, kidney, and ovarian cancer.
- **Interventions:** Guided physical exercise, independent exercise, and atorvastatin.
- **Australian involvement:** ClinicalTrials.gov listed Finland only when checked.
- **Patient relevance:** Broader metastatic-cancer trial; not prostate-specific and not Australian.

## What this changes for patient discussions

### Questions for the oncologist / GP / cardio-oncology team

- Do I have a standard cardiovascular indication for LDL lowering — based on prior cardiovascular disease, LDL level, diabetes, kidney disease, smoking, blood pressure, age, or family history?
- How will ADT, abiraterone/prednisone, enzalutamide, apalutamide, darolutamide, chemotherapy, or steroids affect my cardiovascular risk?
- Should LDL cholesterol, HDL, triglycerides, HbA1c/glucose, blood pressure, weight, waist circumference, and exercise capacity be checked at baseline and during treatment?
- If I am already on a statin, should it be continued during prostate-cancer treatment unless there is a safety reason not to?
- If I cannot tolerate statins, are ezetimibe or PCSK9 inhibitors appropriate for cardiovascular reasons?
- Is the Australian evolocumab/PCPro mCRPC trial relevant to my disease state and treatment line?
- If a trial is suggested, is the goal biomarker modulation, cardiovascular protection, delayed castration resistance, PSA response, radiographic progression-free survival, or overall survival?

## Repository interpretation

LDL-lowering therapy sits at the intersection of **advanced prostate-cancer biology** and **cardiovascular prevention**. The statin evidence in advanced prostate cancer is encouraging but remains mostly observational. PCSK9-inhibitor evidence is much earlier and currently biomarker-directed. The most defensible clinical message is:

1. optimize cardiovascular risk aggressively during ADT and androgen-receptor pathway therapy;
2. continue or start LDL-lowering therapy when there is a standard cardiovascular indication;
3. treat statin/PCSK9 anticancer benefit as **hypothesis-generating** unless randomized prostate-cancer endpoint trials confirm benefit;
4. consider clinical trials, especially the Australian evolocumab/PCPro mCRPC trial, when eligibility and goals fit.
