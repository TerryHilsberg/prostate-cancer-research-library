# Decision Map

For help understanding which specialist is usually involved at each point, see [Clinician Roles in Prostate Cancer Care](clinician-roles.md).

## A. Before diagnosis

- What prompted testing: routine screening, symptoms, an existing abnormal result, or monitoring known cancer?
- Which local guideline, family/genetic/ancestry risk group, health status and life expectancy inform screening? The [2026 ACS update](../../updates/2026-11-16.md) is US guidance, not an automatic replacement for Australian recommendations.
- What is the PSA trend, PSA density, medication/infection context, and need for clinician-directed repeat testing?
- Is MRI and/or a validated biomarker appropriate before biopsy, and what follow-up protects against false reassurance?
- What are the false-positive, false-negative, and overdiagnosis issues?

## B. If biopsy is being considered

- Transperineal vs transrectal approach
- MRI-targeted plus systematic biopsy vs systematic alone
- Infection, bleeding, urinary retention, and pain risks
- Whether antibiotics or anesthesia/sedation are relevant

## C. If cancer is found

- Grade Group/Gleason score
- Number and percentage of positive cores
- PSA, PSA density, MRI lesion size, clinical stage
- Whether genomic classifier or [PSMA PET/CT](../../papers/psma-pet-ct-prostate-cancer-evidence/index.md) is relevant

## D. Management options

- Active surveillance: confirm a written PSA/MRI/biopsy schedule, reassessment triggers, and who tracks overdue monitoring; improved MRI/biomarker risk prediction does not itself prove routine biopsy omission is safe ([2026-11-16 appraisal](../../updates/2026-11-16.md))
- Radical prostatectomy
- For radical prostatectomy: whether structured prehabilitation is available and, in selected high-risk disease, whether perioperative systemic therapy belongs in the multidisciplinary comparison
- External beam radiation therapy: conventional versus moderate hypofractionation versus five-treatment ultrahypofractionation/SBRT, including baseline urinary function and whether pelvic nodes are treated; pelvic-node SBRT is a separate evidence/safety question from prostate-only SBRT, and patient-reported bowel bother matters alongside graded toxicity ([SRAM appraisal](../../updates/2026-11-16.md))
- Brachytherapy
- Focal therapy/investigational approaches
- ADT or systemic therapy for higher-risk/advanced disease

## E. Outcomes to track

- Cancer control and metastasis-free survival
- Need for later treatment
- Urinary incontinence/irritative symptoms
- Sexual function
- Bowel symptoms
- Fatigue, mood, sleep, pain, metabolic health, and quality of life, including baseline assessment and change over time
