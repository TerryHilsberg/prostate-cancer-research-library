## Accepted items

### 1) Patient-reported outcome thresholds: making EORTC QLQ-PR25 scores clinically interpretable

- **Citation:** Vorbach SM, Pilz MJ, Thumfart M, Venderbos LDF, Arraras JI, Bultijnck R, et al. **From patient-reported outcome scores to clinical meaning: defining thresholds for clinical importance for the EORTC QLQ-PR25 questionnaire for patients with prostate cancer.** *Radiotherapy and Oncology.* Published 2026-10-10. DOI: [10.1016/j.radonc.2026.111817](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.radonc.2026.111817). PMID: [42858854](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42858854/).
- **Evidence type and population:** Prospective multicenter patient-reported-outcome study; 261 patients with prostate cancer from seven countries completed EORTC QLQ-PR25 scales plus anchor questions about daily-life limitation, need for help/care, and worries.
- **Categories:** Side effects & quality of life; shared decision-making; treatment follow-up; survivorship.
- **Selection score:** 8.2/10 — directly patient-facing quality-of-life measurement evidence from a prospective multicenter cohort, but it validates interpretation thresholds rather than comparing treatments.
- **Summary:** The study derived thresholds for clinical importance for the prostate-cancer-specific EORTC QLQ-PR25 questionnaire, aiming to make urinary, bowel, hormonal, and sexual symptom scores easier to interpret at the individual-patient level.
- **Main finding:** Five of six QLQ-PR25 scales showed acceptable to excellent discrimination against patient anchors, with AUC values from 0.74 to 0.90. No meaningful threshold could be established for the sexual-activity scale.
- **Patient relevance:** This supports asking care teams not only “what is my score?” but “is this score clinically important, and does it signal need for symptom management, rehabilitation, medication review, pelvic-floor/sexual-health support, or further assessment?”
- **Limitations:** Thresholds depend on the questionnaire, anchors, population, language/cultural context, and treatment mix. The sexual-activity scale result means not every domain can be reduced to a single reliable cutoff.
- **Safety/clinical cautions:** Patient-reported scores should prompt clinician discussion, not self-diagnosis or unsupervised treatment changes. New, severe, or rapidly worsening urinary, bowel, pain, bleeding, fatigue, mood, or sexual symptoms need appropriate clinical review.
- **Repository impact:** Updates the side-effects/QoL category page. No decision-map change, but it strengthens the repository's emphasis on clinically meaningful patient-reported outcomes.

### 2) PSA density velocity before MRI/biopsy: little added value beyond current PSA density, PI-RADS, age, and clinical context

- **Citation:** Narayanasamy S, Kurata Y, Kido A, Nakai H, Takahashi H, LeGout JD, et al. **Utility of PSA density velocity in diagnosing prostate cancer on prostate MR.** *Abdominal Radiology.* Published 2026-10-10. DOI: [10.1097/JU.0000000000003892](https://doi.org/10.1097/JU.0000000000003892). PMID: [42858215](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42858215/).
- **Evidence type and population:** Retrospective diagnostic MRI cohort; 1,174 patients who had at least two prostate MRI examinations 0.3-2 years apart and pathologic confirmation within 1 year after the last MRI, including suspected-cancer and active-surveillance contexts.
- **Categories:** Screening & early detection; diagnostic workup; PSA interpretation; MRI-first pathways; active surveillance.
- **Selection score:** 7.8/10 — clinically relevant diagnostic-pathway evidence with a large MRI denominator, but retrospective and not practice-changing by itself.
- **Summary:** The study asked whether change over time in PSA density adds diagnostic value for clinically significant prostate cancer beyond established variables.
- **Main finding:** PSA density velocity was associated with clinically significant cancer in univariate analysis, but not after multivariable adjustment. Adding PSA density velocity to a model with age, PI-RADS, clinical cohort, and current PSA density did not meaningfully improve optimism-corrected AUC (0.7605 vs 0.7611).
- **Patient relevance:** For a person watching PSA, prostate volume, and MRI results over time, this reinforces that **current PSA density, MRI quality/PI-RADS, age, prior cancer/active-surveillance context, and biopsy/pathology history** remain more central than a new derived “PSA-density velocity” number.
- **Limitations:** Retrospective design; selected patients with repeat MRI and pathology; local MRI quality, radiologist expertise, biopsy route, and follow-up practices may affect generalizability.
- **Safety/clinical cautions:** A reassuring velocity calculation should not override concerning MRI, PSA density, DRE, family/genetic risk, symptoms, or clinician judgment. Conversely, a rising PSA-related metric is not a cancer diagnosis.
- **Repository impact:** Updates diagnostic-workup and active-surveillance watch notes. No decision-map change.

### 3) Sequential biparametric MRI and PRECISE scoring in biopsy-naive men who defer biopsy

- **Citation:** Tian Y, Liu Z, Chen S, Zhou Y, Ruan M, Li D, et al. **PRECISE Assessment of Sequential Biparametric MRI and Clinically Significant Prostate Cancer in Biopsy-naive Men: A Retrospective Cohort Study.** *European Urology Open Science.* Published online 2026-09; November 2026 issue. DOI: [10.1016/j.euros.2026.09.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.euros.2026.09.002). PMID: [42831142](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42831142/).
- **Evidence type and population:** Retrospective single-center cohort; 174 biopsy-naive men with suspected prostate cancer who underwent at least two pre-biopsy biparametric MRI exams separated by at least 12 months, followed by prostate biopsy.
- **Categories:** Diagnostic workup; MRI-first pathways; biopsy decision-making; active surveillance-adjacent monitoring; risk stratification.
- **Selection score:** 7.6/10 — directly relevant to men considering biopsy deferral with MRI follow-up, but small, retrospective, and single-center.
- **Summary:** The study evaluated whether PRECISE version 2 scoring of MRI change over time is associated with clinically significant cancer at later biopsy.
- **Main finding:** Clinically significant cancer rates rose across PRECISE categories: 6.1% for PRECISE 2/3 non-visible, 26% for PRECISE 3 visible, and 81% for PRECISE 4-5. After adjustment, repeat PSA density and PRECISE 4-5 remained associated with clinically significant cancer.
- **Patient relevance:** If biopsy is deferred after MRI, this supports asking how repeat MRI changes are being interpreted, whether a standardized change score such as PRECISE is used, and how repeat PSA density influences the biopsy discussion.
- **Limitations:** Not randomized; single center; only 174 men; all eventually had biopsy, so the cohort may not represent all men monitored after MRI; biparametric MRI protocols and reader expertise may vary.
- **Safety/clinical cautions:** MRI surveillance after suspected prostate cancer is not the same as being “cleared.” Deferring biopsy requires explicit follow-up intervals, triggers for biopsy, safety-netting for PSA/MRI changes, and clinician review.
- **Repository impact:** Updates diagnostic-workup and active-surveillance watch notes. No decision-map change, but adds a clinician-discussion prompt for repeat-MRI interpretation.

### 4) Incisional hernia after minimally invasive radical prostatectomy: under-recognized surgical trade-off

- **Citation:** Peyrottes A, Oberlin O, Baboudjian M, Dariane C, Panthier F, Mongiat-Artus P, et al. **Incisional Hernia After Minimally Invasive Radical Prostatectomy: A Systematic Review.** *European Urology Open Science.* Published online 2026-08; November 2026 issue. DOI: [10.1016/j.euros.2026.08.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.euros.2026.08.002). PMID: [42859558](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42859558/).
- **Evidence type and population:** Systematic review of 16 studies published 2010-2025, including 56,661 patients undergoing minimally invasive radical prostatectomy.
- **Categories:** Localized treatment; surgery; side effects & quality of life; survivorship.
- **Selection score:** 8.0/10 — systematic review of a practical surgical complication that patients may not hear much about, limited by heterogeneous retrospective evidence.
- **Summary:** The review assessed frequency, risk factors, and surgical determinants of incisional hernia after minimally invasive radical prostatectomy.
- **Main finding:** Reported incisional-hernia proportions ranged widely from 0.04% to 31%, with median diagnosis around 14 months. Repair was required in up to 80% of cases in included studies, and emergency repair in up to 15%. Available observational comparisons suggested lower hernia proportions with transverse suprapubic/Pfannenstiel specimen extraction than periumbilical extraction, but evidence quality was limited.
- **Patient relevance:** For men comparing surgery with radiation, focal therapy, or surveillance, this adds a concrete “abdominal wall/hernia” question to the usual continence, erectile-function, bowel, cancer-control, and recovery-time discussion.
- **Limitations:** Mostly retrospective studies; variable hernia definitions, diagnostic methods, follow-up, patient risk factors, surgical technique, and reporting; the review does not prove that one extraction site is best for every patient.
- **Safety/clinical cautions:** Hernia risk depends on obesity, prior abdominal surgery, wound infection/abscess, prostate size/specimen extraction, healing factors, and surgeon technique. Patients should ask their surgeon what warning signs require urgent review after prostatectomy.
- **Repository impact:** Updates localized-treatment and side-effects/QoL pages. No decision-map change, but adds a surgical-complication counseling point.

### 5) PSMA PET/CT kinetics during treatment: promising but not validated as a response-assessment tool

- **Citation:** Le Guévelou J, Bauckneht M, Soeterik TFW, Scheltema M, Bianchi L, Kesch C, et al. **PSMA PET/CT Kinetics During Systemic Therapy and Radiotherapy in Hormone-sensitive Prostate Cancer: A Systematic Review.** *European Urology Open Science.* Published online 2026-09; November 2026 issue. DOI: [10.1016/j.euros.2026.09.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.euros.2026.09.003). PMID: [42828270](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42828270/).
- **Evidence type and population:** Systematic review of 11 studies, including two prospective single-arm studies and nine retrospective studies, evaluating PSMA PET/CT expression changes during systemic therapy or after radiotherapy in hormone-sensitive prostate cancer.
- **Categories:** PSMA PET/imaging; advanced & recurrent disease; radiotherapy; treatment monitoring; biomarkers.
- **Selection score:** 7.7/10 — important imaging watch item because PSMA PET is increasingly used, but evidence remains immature for response assessment.
- **Summary:** The review synthesized how PSMA PET uptake changes during androgen deprivation/systemic therapy and after radiotherapy.
- **Main finding:** PSMA expression often decreased under ADT, but one prospective study observed early “flare” in some lesions at 3-4 weeks. The authors emphasize that PSMA PET/CT response assessment in hormone-sensitive prostate cancer is **not yet validated** for oncologic outcomes.
- **Patient relevance:** Helps patients ask whether a post-treatment PSMA PET result is being used for standard staging/restaging, trial-based monitoring, or an experimental/individualized response assessment.
- **Limitations:** Few studies; mostly retrospective; different tracers, time points, lesion metrics, treatments, and outcome links; limited evidence tying early imaging kinetics to survival or durable disease control.
- **Safety/clinical cautions:** A changing PSMA PET signal should not be interpreted in isolation. Timing after ADT or radiotherapy can matter, and apparent flare or decreased uptake may not equal clinical progression or cure. Treatment changes should be based on multidisciplinary clinical review.
- **Repository impact:** Updates imaging/biomarkers and advanced/recurrent-disease watch notes. No decision-map change.

## Items excluded or held for watchlist

- Preclinical molecular, imaging-probe, drug-delivery, and cell-line studies were excluded because they do not yet change patient diagnostic or treatment decisions.
- Case reports, surgical technique videos, bibliometric analyses, and small single-institution salvage-treatment series were excluded unless they raised a broad safety issue.
- A US cost-effectiveness model comparing androgen-receptor inhibitor doublets in metastatic castration-sensitive prostate cancer was held for watchlist because it is payer-context-specific, model-dependent, and less directly decision-changing than guideline/trial evidence.
- A salvage proton-therapy series after cryosurgery/HIFU was held for watchlist because it involved only 31 patients, although it may be useful background for future focal-therapy recurrence pages.

## Practical clinician-discussion prompts from this week

- **Quality-of-life monitoring:** “If I complete a QLQ-PR25 or similar questionnaire, what score change or threshold would be clinically meaningful enough to trigger supportive care?”
- **PSA/MRI interpretation:** “In my case, how much weight should we give current PSA density, PSA trend, prostate volume, PI-RADS score, repeat MRI change, and biopsy history?”
- **Biopsy deferral:** “If we defer biopsy after MRI, what follow-up interval, repeat-MRI criteria, PSA-density trigger, and safety-net plan will we use?”
- **Surgery counseling:** “For minimally invasive prostatectomy, what is my incisional-hernia risk, where is the specimen usually extracted, and what symptoms after surgery need urgent review?”
- **PSMA PET monitoring:** “Is this PSMA PET scan being used for validated staging/restaging, or for treatment-response monitoring where evidence is still emerging?”
