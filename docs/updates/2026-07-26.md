## Accepted items

### 1) Active surveillance for selected intermediate-risk prostate cancer

- **Citation:** Uleri A, Cella L, Long-Depaquit T, Sordelli F, et al. **Active surveillance for intermediate-risk prostate cancer: a systematic review and pooled analysis.** *BJU International.* Published 2026-07-26. DOI: [10.1111/bju.70388](https://doi.org/10.1111/bju.70388). PMID: [42503039](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42503039/)
- **Evidence type and population:** Systematic review and pooled analysis; 18 studies including 3,783 selected patients with intermediate-risk prostate cancer, including Gleason Grade Group 1–2 / Grade Group 2 cohorts.
- **Category/categories:** Active Surveillance; Risk Stratification & Staging; Diagnostic Workup; Shared Decision-Making.
- **Selection score:** 8.6/10.
- **Summary:** The review pooled outcomes for active surveillance in selected intermediate-risk disease. Pooled active-surveillance discontinuation-free survival was 70% at 3 years, 63% at 5 years, and 53% at 10 years. In Grade Group 2 cohorts, corresponding estimates were 72%, 56%, and 40%. Upgrade-free survival to Grade Group ≥3 was higher in cohorts screened using multiparametric MRI.
- **Main finding:** Active surveillance may be a reasonable discussion option for carefully selected intermediate-risk patients, especially when modern MRI and biopsy selection/follow-up are used, but long-term robust cancer-control data remain limited.
- **Patient relevance:** A patient told they have “favorable intermediate-risk” or limited Grade Group 2 disease can ask whether active surveillance is being considered, what makes them eligible or ineligible, and how MRI, biopsy extent, PSA density, tumour volume, family history/genetics, and personal tolerance for uncertainty are being weighed.
- **Limitations:** The included studies had heterogeneous surveillance protocols, moderate risk of bias, and limited long-term metastasis/prostate-cancer-specific mortality data. Results should not be generalized to all intermediate-risk disease, particularly higher-volume Grade Group 2, unfavorable intermediate-risk, Grade Group ≥3, concerning MRI features, or adverse genomic/pathology features.
- **Safety/clinical cautions:** Active surveillance is not “doing nothing.” It requires a structured monitoring plan and a clear trigger for repeat MRI, biopsy, or treatment. Delaying treatment may be inappropriate for some intermediate-risk patients; decisions should be made with urology/radiation oncology input.
- **Changes to decision map/category page:** **Yes.** Filed under Active Surveillance as a high-relevance update; it adds nuance rather than a blanket recommendation.

### 2) TALAPRO-2 post hoc analysis in HRR non-deficient or unknown mCRPC

- **Citation:** Yajima S, Yoshida S, Chen W, Fukushima H, et al. **Exploratory Time-Dependent Patterns in Estimated Treatment Effect of Talazoparib Plus Enzalutamide in HRR Non-Deficient or Unknown Metastatic Castration-Resistant Prostate Cancer: A Post Hoc Analysis of the TALAPRO-2 Trial.** *International Journal of Urology.* Published July 2026. DOI: [10.1111/iju.70582](https://doi.org/10.1111/iju.70582). PMID: [42502892](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42502892/). ClinicalTrials.gov: [NCT03395197](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03395197)
- **Evidence type and population:** Post hoc reconstructed individual-patient-data analysis from published Kaplan–Meier curves; 636 patients in the homologous-recombination-repair (HRR) non-deficient or unknown subgroup of TALAPRO-2 with metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer.
- **Category/categories:** Advanced & Recurrent Disease; Therapies; Biomarkers/Genomics; Shared Decision-Making.
- **Selection score:** 7.7/10, included because it is tied to a major randomized trial and a high-stakes treatment-selection issue.
- **Summary:** The analysis explored whether estimated effects of talazoparib plus enzalutamide varied over time in patients without confirmed HRR deficiency or with unknown HRR status. It did not find formal statistical evidence of a time-varying treatment effect; the authors emphasized that the findings are descriptive and hypothesis-generating.
- **Main finding:** For patients without confirmed HRR-deficient disease, the paper does **not** establish a clear new survival-selection rule. It reinforces that PARP-inhibitor combination discussions should be biomarker-aware and should distinguish overall trial results from subgroup certainty.
- **Patient relevance:** Patients with metastatic castration-resistant disease can ask whether germline and tumour HRR testing has been done, whether their cancer has a BRCA1/2 or other HRR alteration, and how expected benefit, anemia/cytopenias, fatigue, falls, drug interactions, cost/access, and alternatives compare for their biomarker subgroup.
- **Limitations:** This was a post hoc analysis using reconstructed rather than original trial-level patient data. Nominal interval findings should not be treated as definitive, and the results should not be extrapolated to molecularly confirmed HRR-proficient disease without specialist interpretation.
- **Safety/clinical cautions:** Talazoparib/enzalutamide can add hematologic toxicity, fatigue, falls, hypertension, drug-interaction and quality-of-life burdens. Treatment selection should be reviewed by medical oncology with genomic context and monitoring plans.
- **Changes to decision map/category page:** **Yes.** Filed under Advanced & Recurrent Disease as biomarker-aware treatment-selection context; it does not change a patient-facing recommendation.

### 3) Salivary-gland imaging changes after lutetium-177 PSMA radioligand therapy

- **Citation:** Köylüce N, Demir F. **Quantitative Imaging Evaluation of Structural and Densitometric Changes in Major Salivary Glands After 177Lu PSMA Radioligand Therapy.** *Annals of Nuclear Medicine.* Published 2026-07-26. DOI: [10.1007/s12149-026-02266-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12149-026-02266-1). PMID: [42503048](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42503048/)
- **Evidence type and population:** Retrospective imaging study; 68 patients with metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer treated with 177Lu-PSMA-617 radioligand therapy.
- **Category/categories:** Advanced & Recurrent Disease; Imaging & Biomarkers; Therapies; Side Effects & Quality of Life.
- **Selection score:** 7.6/10, included for treatment-toxicity and monitoring relevance.
- **Summary:** The study measured PET/CT-derived structural and uptake changes in parotid and submandibular glands before and after PSMA radioligand therapy. It found significant post-treatment reductions in salivary-gland size/activity measures, especially in submandibular glands; exploratory associations with number of cycles or cumulative activity did not remain significant after multiple-testing correction.
- **Main finding:** The paper adds imaging evidence that salivary glands can undergo measurable changes after 177Lu-PSMA therapy, consistent with the clinical concern about dry mouth/salivary toxicity, but it does not define a reliable individual risk-prediction rule.
- **Patient relevance:** Patients considering or receiving PSMA radioligand therapy can ask about expected dry-mouth risk, dental/oral-health precautions, hydration/saliva-management strategies, symptom monitoring, and how salivary toxicity is balanced against cancer-control goals.
- **Limitations:** Small retrospective study, imaging endpoints rather than full patient-reported dry-mouth outcomes, and no corrected predictor of who will develop worse gland changes. Findings apply to treated mCRPC patients and may not generalize to earlier-disease trials or different protocols.
- **Safety/clinical cautions:** Radioligand therapy requires nuclear-medicine/oncology oversight, PSMA-avid disease confirmation, marrow/renal/salivary monitoring, radiation-safety procedures, and discussion of fatigue, nausea, cytopenias, kidney function, and oral/dental complications.
- **Changes to decision map/category page:** **Yes.** Filed under Advanced & Recurrent Disease and Side Effects & Quality of Life as a PSMA-therapy monitoring issue.

### 4) YouTube quality gaps for robot-assisted radical prostatectomy information

- **Citation:** Gezmiş CT, Çakıroğlu B. **YouTube as a source of patient information on robot-assisted radical prostatectomy: a cross-sectional study of quality, reliability, transparency, and content gaps.** *Therapeutic Advances in Urology.* Published 2026. DOI: [10.1177/17562872261469665](https://doi.org/10.1177/17562872261469665). PMID: [42502269](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42502269/)
- **Evidence type and population:** Cross-sectional evaluation of 174 YouTube videos about robot-assisted radical prostatectomy, using quality/reliability/transparency metrics and a prostatectomy counseling checklist.
- **Category/categories:** Localized Treatment; Side Effects & Quality of Life; Shared Decision-Making; Patient Education.
- **Selection score:** 7.5/10, included because it directly affects patient information quality and shared decision-making.
- **Summary:** Most videos targeted patients, but median educational quality/reliability scores were low to moderate and transparency was limited. Important counseling topics were often underrepresented, including postoperative PSA follow-up, complication rates, erectile dysfunction, urinary continence outcomes, and recovery timeline.
- **Main finding:** Popular online surgical videos may be useful as orientation, but they often omit the very issues patients need for informed consent and comparison with radiation, active surveillance, focal therapy, or other pathways.
- **Patient relevance:** Before relying on a video, patients can check whether it discusses baseline function, cancer risk group, continence/pad rates, erectile recovery and rehabilitation, complications, PSA follow-up, surgeon/team outcomes, recovery timeline, alternatives, and uncertainties.
- **Limitations:** YouTube content changes rapidly; search terms, geography, language, platform algorithms, and upload dates affect results. The study evaluates information quality, not clinical outcomes of surgery.
- **Safety/clinical cautions:** Online videos should not replace clinician counseling or individualized consent. Patients should bring questions from videos to their urologist/radiation oncologist rather than treating promotional or anecdotal content as balanced evidence.
- **Changes to decision map/category page:** **Yes.** Filed under Shared Decision-Making and Side Effects & Quality of Life as an information-quality caution.

## Overall interpretation for patients

This week’s strongest patient-facing signal is that **selection quality matters**: active surveillance may be reasonable for some favorable intermediate-risk patients, but only with careful MRI/biopsy/risk-feature selection and structured follow-up. In advanced disease, new analyses continue to emphasize biomarker-aware treatment discussions and toxicity monitoring rather than simple one-size-fits-all choices. Online prostatectomy videos should be treated as prompts for clinician questions, not as substitutes for balanced counseling.
