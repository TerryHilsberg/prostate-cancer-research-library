    2026-09-28 — Weekly Prostate Cancer Literature & Guideline Scan

    Educational research support only — not medical advice. Use these notes to prepare questions for a qualified urologist, radiation oncologist, medical oncologist, radiologist, GP, specialist nurse, pelvic-floor physiotherapist, or other relevant clinician.

    Scan scope

    • Run date: 2026-09-28
    • Look-back window: primarily 2026-09-14 to 2026-09-28, with priority to newly published or newly indexed higher-quality evidence.
    • Sources checked: PubMed/NCBI, Crossref metadata checks for suspicious PubMed DOI/journal fields, and guideline watch pages for NCCN, EAU, AUA, ASCO, NICE, and Cancer Australia.
    • Selection threshold: included items scored ≥ 7.5/10 using the repository selection policy. Lower-quality mechanistic, case-report, small retrospective, commercial-only, or press-release-only items were excluded unless needed for safety/guideline watch.

    Accepted items

    1) Short-course SBRT vs moderately hypofractionated IMRT for localized intermediate-risk disease

    • Citation: Ellis RJ, Pugh SL, Yu JB, et al. Stereotactic Body Radiotherapy vs Moderately Hypofractionated IMRT for Localized Intermediate-Risk Prostate Cancer: A Randomized Clinical Trial. JAMA. Published 2026-09-22. DOI: 10.1001/jama.2026.12627. PMID: 42593775. Trial: NCT03367702.
    • Evidence type and population: Phase 3 international randomized trial, 698 men with localized intermediate-risk prostate cancer treated at 136 centers; SBRT 36.25 Gy in 5 fractions vs moderately hypofractionated IMRT 70 Gy in 28 fractions or 60 Gy in 20 fractions.
    • Categories: Localized treatment; side effects & quality of life; shared decision-making.
    • Selection score: 9.5/10 — randomized, directly patient-facing treatment-choice evidence with quality-of-life and disease-control endpoints.
    • Summary: The trial tested whether a 5-treatment SBRT schedule was superior to a longer moderately hypofractionated IMRT schedule for urinary/bowel quality of life and disease-free survival.
    • Main finding: SBRT improved several patient-reported quality-of-life measures, including fewer bowel-domain clinically meaningful declines at 2 years, and had fewer grade 3-4 genitourinary adverse events. It was not superior to MH-IMRT for disease-free survival at 3 years; the published abstract reports 3-year DFS of 92.1% with MH-IMRT vs 88.6% with SBRT.
    • Patient relevance: For a patient comparing radiation options, this supports asking about convenience/time burden, urinary/bowel/sexual trade-offs, expected local control, dose schedule, prostate size/anatomy, baseline urinary symptoms, rectal/bladder constraints, and clinician/center experience.
    • Limitations: Open-label design; median follow-up 3.2 years; applies to trial-eligible localized intermediate-risk groups and the tested SBRT dose/fractionation, not every SBRT protocol or high-risk setting.
    • Safety/clinical cautions: A shorter course is not automatically the better course. Radiation planning quality, image guidance, organs-at-risk constraints, urinary baseline, bowel comorbidity, and salvage options matter. Discuss with a radiation oncologist; do not switch, start, or delay treatment based on this summary alone.
    • Repository impact: Updates localized-treatment and side-effect/QoL category watch items; does not overturn the decision map, but strengthens the “radiotherapy schedule and quality-of-life trade-off” discussion.

    2) Multimodal MRI + PSMA PET/CT-guided biopsy safety and patient-reported outcomes

    • Citation: Schmidt CJ, Reichert T, Gaertner FC, et al. Safety and patient-reported outcomes of multimodal PSMA PET/MRI-guided prostate biopsy: results from the prospective multicenter DEPROMP trial. World Journal of Urology. Published 2026-09-17. DOI: 10.1007/s00345-026-06767-6. PMID: 42752988.
    • Evidence type and population: Prospective multicenter DEPROMP trial safety/PRO analysis; 230 biopsy-naïve men with suspected prostate cancer underwent systematic, MRI-targeted, and PSMA PET/CT-targeted transrectal biopsy.
    • Categories: Diagnostic workup; imaging & biomarkers; biopsy; shared decision-making; side effects & quality of life.
    • Selection score: 8.2/10 — prospective multicenter patient-centered safety evidence in a modern image-guided pathway.
    • Summary: The study followed adverse events and patient-reported outcomes after multimodal image-guided prostate biopsy.
    • Main finding: Adverse events were infrequent and mostly low grade; 94.8% had no symptoms immediately post-biopsy and 87.4% were symptom-free at 6 months. At 6 months, quality-of-life worsening was attributed more often to the cancer diagnosis than to biopsy-related adverse events.
    • Patient relevance: Helps patients ask separate questions about procedural harm, infection prevention, route choice, core number, imaging targets, and the psychological burden of receiving a diagnosis.
    • Limitations: The biopsy route was transrectal; results may not apply to transperineal pathways, lower-resource settings, centers without PSMA PET integration, or patients with higher infection risk.
    • Safety/clinical cautions: Biopsy route, antibiotic strategy, local infection patterns, anticoagulants, urinary retention risk, and anesthesia/tolerability need clinician review. PSMA PET-targeted biopsy is not yet a universal first-line pathway.
    • Repository impact: Updates diagnostic-workup and imaging/biomarker category watch items; no decision-map change.

    3) ADT and acute kidney injury: systematic review/meta-analysis

    • Citation: Pires IZ, Fujimura AY, da Silva Gobbo MO, et al. Association of androgen deprivation therapy and acute kidney injury in patients with prostate cancer: a systematic review and meta-analysis of 72,980 patients. Cancer Chemotherapy and Pharmacology. Published 2026-09-24. DOI: 10.1007/s00280-026-04962-1. PMID: 42786231.
    • Evidence type and population: Systematic review/meta-analysis of 4 observational studies, 72,980 patients, comparing acute kidney injury odds in prostate cancer patients receiving ADT vs no ADT.
    • Categories: Advanced & recurrent disease; localized treatment when ADT is added to radiotherapy; side effects & quality of life; medical safety.
    • Selection score: 8.0/10 — large safety-focused synthesis, but limited by observational evidence and heterogeneity.
    • Summary: The review evaluated whether ADT exposure is associated with acute kidney injury.
    • Main finding: ADT was associated with higher odds of AKI overall (OR 1.34, 95% CI 1.29-1.40), with a signal for GnRH agonist-based therapy. The authors emphasize observational limitations and methodological heterogeneity.
    • Patient relevance: For patients considering ADT with radiation, biochemical recurrence treatment, metastatic hormone-sensitive treatment, or long-term systemic therapy, kidney function should be part of the baseline and follow-up safety conversation alongside cardiovascular, metabolic, bone, hot-flash, sexual, fatigue, and mood effects.
    • Limitations: Observational included studies cannot prove causation; residual confounding is likely; only 4 studies; ADT modality comparisons may be affected by selection bias and different clinical contexts.
    • Safety/clinical cautions: Do not stop or avoid indicated ADT because of this review. Discuss baseline creatinine/eGFR, diabetes, blood pressure, dehydration risk, NSAIDs, contrast exposure, urinary obstruction, other nephrotoxic medicines, and monitoring frequency with clinicians.
    • Repository impact: Updates advanced/recurrent-disease and side-effect/QoL category watch items; no decision-map change.

    4) Individualized PSA surveillance after radical prostatectomy

    • Citation: Pellegrino F, Falagario UG, Abbadi A, et al. Individualized Oncologic Surveillance After Radical Prostatectomy for Localized Prostate Cancer. European Urology Oncology. Published 2026-09-25. DOI: 10.1016/j.euo.2026.09.009. PMID: 42791144.
    • Evidence type and population: Retrospective population-based modeling study of 11,552 patients after radical prostatectomy.
    • Categories: Localized treatment; recurrence surveillance; risk stratification/staging; shared decision-making.
    • Selection score: 7.8/10 — large, clinically relevant surveillance modeling study, but not yet a validated protocol-changing trial.
    • Summary: The study modeled follow-up intensity after prostatectomy using biochemical recurrence risk, high-risk recurrence definitions, age, comorbidity, and competing mortality risk.
    • Main finding: The authors propose more intensive early follow-up for patients with adverse pathological features, de-intensification later, and less frequent follow-up for favorable-feature patients. They state that future validation is needed before implementation.
    • Patient relevance: Supports asking what the PSA surveillance schedule is based on: pathology, margins/nodes, Grade Group, PSA kinetics, age, comorbidity, anxiety/logistics, and what PSA threshold would trigger imaging or salvage-treatment discussion.
    • Limitations: Retrospective modeling; health-system specific; not a randomized comparison of surveillance schedules; does not replace existing EAU/NCCN/AUA follow-up guidance.
    • Safety/clinical cautions: Do not reduce PSA follow-up frequency without the treating clinician’s agreement. Early salvage opportunities can depend on timely PSA detection.
    • Repository impact: Updates localized-treatment follow-up watch; no category-page decision change beyond filed item.

    5) AI risk classification on biparametric MRI for biopsy triage

    • Citation: Guenzel K, Poirot MG, van Loon A, et al. Multicentre evaluation of artificial intelligence risk classification for detection of clinically significant prostate cancer on biparametric MRI. European Radiology. Published 2026-09-26. DOI: 10.1007/s00330-026-12888-8. PMID: 42800842.
    • Evidence type and population: Retrospective multicenter, multivendor diagnostic-accuracy study; 787 men with prostate MRI and histopathologic verification; clinically significant cancer defined as ISUP Grade Group ≥2.
    • Categories: Diagnostic workup; imaging & biomarkers; AI/integrated decision support; biopsy triage.
    • Selection score: 7.7/10 — multicenter diagnostic evidence directly relevant to MRI-first pathways, but retrospective and product-specific.
    • Summary: The study compared a commercial AI risk-classification system with PI-RADS for clinically significant cancer detection and modeled biopsy-avoidance strategies, especially for PI-RADS 3 lesions.
    • Main finding: AI was non-inferior to PI-RADS for patient-level sensitivity but not lesion-level sensitivity. A modeled targeted strategy for PI-RADS 3 avoided 18.9% of biopsies while maintaining 98.4% sensitivity. The authors position AI as decision support, not a replacement for radiologists.
    • Patient relevance: Useful for asking whether an MRI report used AI support, whether the radiologist remains responsible for lesion mapping, and how PI-RADS 3 decisions are integrated with PSA density, prostate volume, family/genetic risk, symptoms, prior biopsy, and patient preferences.
    • Limitations: Retrospective design; histopathology-verification selection effects; commercial AI system; external prospective clinical-impact validation still needed.
    • Safety/clinical cautions: AI “low risk” should not be treated as a cancer rule-out. Clinician interpretation and follow-up safeguards remain essential.
    • Repository impact: Updates diagnostic-workup and imaging/biomarker category watch items; no decision-map change.

    Items excluded or held for watchlist

    • Small mechanistic studies on resistance biology, ferroptosis, epigenetics, or nanoparticle therapy were excluded as preclinical/early translational and not yet patient-decision changing.
    • Small, retrospective, or case-report imaging/therapy papers were excluded unless they raised a direct safety issue.
    • Recently published supplement/repurposing studies, including grape-seed extract for PSA-only progression, were not included as practice-changing evidence; these remain clinician-discussion/watchlist items only and should not be used to substitute for standard evaluation or therapy.
    • The 2026-09-14 pelvic-floor/biofeedback randomized trial and radical-prostatectomy prehabilitation meta-analysis were not republished because they were already covered in the 2026-09-21 weekly scan.

    Practical clinician-discussion prompts from this week

    • If considering radiation for localized intermediate-risk disease: “How do SBRT and moderately hypofractionated IMRT compare for my anatomy, urinary symptoms, bowel risks, travel burden, and expected disease control?”
    • If entering an MRI-first diagnostic pathway: “Would PSMA PET/CT or AI-assisted MRI add useful information in my case, or would it risk overcomplicating decisions without changing management?”
    • If ADT is being discussed: “What baseline and follow-up kidney, cardiovascular, metabolic, bone, sexual, fatigue, and mood monitoring is appropriate for me?”
    • After radical prostatectomy: “What features determine my PSA follow-up schedule, and what PSA result would trigger imaging or salvage-treatment discussion?”

    Medical safety

    These notes are educational research support, not medical advice. Prostate cancer decisions depend on PSA history, MRI quality, biopsy/pathology, Grade Group, staging, symptoms, age, comorbidities, medications, values, local expertise, and access. Discuss diagnosis, treatment, surveillance, imaging, medication, supplement, or monitoring changes with qualified clinicians.
